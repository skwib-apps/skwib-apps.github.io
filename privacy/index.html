British Coins — Privacy Policy

Effective date: 26 October 2026.

British Coins ("the app") is an offline catalogue of British coins made by skwib apps ("we"). This policy explains what happens to your data when you use the app. Contact: skwib.apps@gmail.com.

In short

  • You do not create an account. The app has no server of its own.
  • Your collection and your settings stay on your device.
  • The app shows ads from Google AdMob. In the EU, the UK and other regions where required, the app asks for your consent first.
  • Crash reports (Firebase Crashlytics) and usage statistics (Google Analytics for Firebase) help us fix errors and improve the app. You can turn each of them off in Settings. In the EU and the UK, statistics are collected only with your consent.
  • We do not ask for your name or e-mail, and we do not sell data.

Data on your device

The catalogue (coins, photos, descriptions) is built into the app and is the same for everyone.

Your collection — the coins you own and want, the grade, price, date and notes you enter — and your settings (language, theme, photo options, the switches below) are stored only on your device. We do not receive them, and they are not part of crash reports or statistics.

System backup. If backup is turned on for your device, Android (Google) or iOS (iCloud) may include your collection and settings in the backup of your device, under the terms of your Google or Apple account. The app excludes its catalogue copy and its encryption key from backups. You manage backups in the settings of your device.

Export files. When you export your collection (a JSON file or a CSV table), the file is created on your device and you choose where to send or save it — for example, to a cloud drive, an e-mail or a messenger. From then on, the file is handled by the service you chose. Importing reads only the file you pick.

Deleting. Uninstalling the app deletes your collection and settings from the device (copies in your backups or export files stay where you keep them).

Advertising (Google AdMob)

The app shows a banner from Google AdMob on some screens. To show ads, the Google Mobile Ads SDK may collect and process information such as:

  • an advertising identifier of your device (Android advertising ID, app set ID; on iOS the IDFA only if you allow tracking),
  • your IP address and approximate location derived from it,
  • information about the device and the app, its performance, and how you interact with the ads.

Google uses this information to show ads, to measure them and to prevent fraud.

Consent. In the European Economic Area, the UK, Switzerland and other regions where required, the app asks for your consent through Google's consent form (User Messaging Platform) before ads are requested. You can change your choice at any time in Settings → Ads and consent (shown where the consent form applies). Without consent for personalised ads you may see non-personalised (limited) ads or no ads at all; the catalogue works the same.

iOS. If the app asks for permission to track (App Tracking Transparency), you can refuse it; the app works the same. You can change it later in the iOS settings (Privacy & Security → Tracking).

The ads are limited to content suitable for general audiences (rating PG).

Crash reports (Firebase Crashlytics)

If the app crashes or meets a serious error, it sends a crash report to Firebase Crashlytics (Google) so that we can find and fix the problem. A report contains:

  • the technical details of the error (the stack trace),
  • the device model, the operating system and its version, the orientation, free memory and storage,
  • the version of the app and of the catalogue data, the language of the app and the type of screen that was open (for example "coin card" — not which coin),
  • a random identifier of the installation (Crashlytics / Firebase installation ID), used to count how many installations an error affects.

A report does not contain your collection, your notes, your search text, your name or e-mail. Your IP address is used to send the report.

Crash reports are sent only from the published versions of the app. They are on by default; you can turn them off in Settings → Send crash reports — then nothing is sent, and reports not yet sent are deleted. Crashlytics keeps crash reports for 90 days.

Usage statistics (Google Analytics for Firebase)

To understand which parts of the app are used, the app sends usage statistics to Google Analytics for Firebase (Google):

  • which screens are opened (by the type of screen, for example "search" or "coin card" — not which coin),
  • actions, without their content: a search was made (only the kinds of terms recognised, such as "year" or "monarch", whether filters were used and the number of results as a range — never the text you typed); a coin was added to the collection; whether a grade or a price was filled in (yes / no — never the values); the wish list was changed; the collection was exported or imported (the format and the result); a setting was changed (which one and its new value),
  • the language and theme of the app and whether it is a store version or a test version,
  • data that Google Analytics collects by itself: a random identifier of the app installation (app instance ID), the device model, the operating system, the app version, sessions (start and duration) and the approximate location (country, region, city) derived from your IP address.

Where ad storage is allowed (in the EU and the UK only with your consent), Google Analytics may also receive the advertising identifier of your device (on iOS only if you allow tracking).

Statistics are sent only from the published versions of the app:

  • in the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland — only if you agree in Google's consent form (the consent to analytics storage); you can change it in Settings → Ads and consent;
  • in other regions — by default;
  • everywhere you can turn them off in Settings → Usage statistics.

We see the statistics only as reports; we do not use them to identify you. Google Analytics keeps event-level data for 2 months (the default setting), after that only aggregated reports remain.

Google as a service provider

AdMob, Firebase Crashlytics and Google Analytics for Firebase are services of Google. Google processes the data described above under its own terms and policies:

  • Google Privacy Policy
  • How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services
  • Advertising at Google
  • Privacy and security in Firebase

Data is sent to Google over encrypted connections (HTTPS) and may be processed in other countries, including the United States.

Legal bases (EEA and UK)

  • Ads and usage statistics: your consent, given in Google's consent form; non-personalised (limited) ads — our legitimate interest in funding a free app.
  • Crash reports: our legitimate interest in keeping the app working; you can object at any time with the switch in Settings.
  • Writing to the author: your message and our answer to it.

Your choices

  • Settings → Ads and consent — change your consent (where the consent form applies).
  • Settings → Send crash reports and Settings → Usage statistics — turn them off.
  • Reset or delete the advertising identifier in the settings of your device (Android: Google → Ads; iOS: Privacy & Security → Tracking).
  • Uninstall the app to delete its data from your device.

Because the app has no accounts, we cannot find which data in these services belongs to you. You can still write to us with any question about your data.

Children

The app is not directed at children.

Writing to the author

If you write to us from the app ("Write to the author"), your e-mail app prepares a message with the version of the app and of the catalogue, the language and the model and system of your device, so that we can understand the problem. You can see and change this text before sending. We use your message only to answer you.
